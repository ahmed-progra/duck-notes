I’m introducing Duck Notes, an open-source Markdown notes app for Android, designed for writing, organizing, and connecting ideas offline.

For students, researchers, and developers, useful notes often extend beyond a single document. A reading list leads to a question; a question develops into an observation; an observation becomes the starting point for a draft. Duck Notes supports that process with wiki links and backlinks, alongside nested tags and search across note titles and content.

The app includes:
• Markdown editing, task lists, and image attachments.
• Nested and pinned tags, Archive, and reversible Trash.
• Wiki links and backlinks for navigating related ideas.
• English and Arabic localization, right-to-left layouts, and light, dark, or system themes.
• Optional device-credential app lock, with screenshot and Recents-preview protection.

Notes are stored locally, and the app requires neither an account nor an internet connection. App lock controls access to the interface; it does not provide separate file encryption.

Built with Kotlin, Jetpack Compose, and Room, Duck Notes is available under the MIT License. Version 0.1.1 brings security and reliability improvements alongside the updated visual identity.
